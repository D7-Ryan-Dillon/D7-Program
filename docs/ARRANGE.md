# The Arrange tab

One building, composed from the tiles, judged as architecture, kept, edited freely, shown and exported.

## The rules that shape everything
- **One connected, walkable building.** Every piece must touch the main set (the biggest group of touching pieces) and be reachable on foot from the entrance (a joint with an opening of 4 ft² or more meeting an opening, with a floor and clearance on both sides). The generator cannot make an island; manual edits that would cut pieces off open a dialog (remove them too / re-attach them / cancel); Add as tile and every export are blocked while anything is detached or overlapping.
- **Free placement, smart snapping.** Positions are multiples of 0.5 ft (the voxel size), not locked to a 20 ft grid. Near another piece a moved piece snaps flush, then opening to opening, then floor to floor (floor 1 against floor 2 is fine), else the half-foot. **Lattice mode** locks to 10 ft.
- **Any box size.** A tile is a box of whole cells (`tileFt`, any width/depth/height; 20 x 40 x 20 or 20 x 20 x 40 work, tested in `npm run check:arrange`). Tiles at another cell size and the Builder's hex prisms cannot be placed (greyed out in the Bank).
- **Orientation**: mirror in X, then quarter turns about the vertical axis (8 orientations), plus optional uniform scale behind the Advanced switch. No tilt or wall mode.

## The generator (Generate panel)
It builds a whole building by **planning the shape, then searching for tiles that satisfy the floor rules in it**. There is no time limit: it runs until the building is made (progress and a Stop button are in the panel; Stop changes nothing).

**What you choose.** *Pieces*; the *kind of building* (all seven: Compact, Tower, Long bar, Courtyard, Terraced, Bridge, Free-form); the *Branching wings* switch; four *feel* sliders: **Tall** (how many levels), **Compact** (tight or loose), **Varied** (a few tiles repeated, or every tile different), **Program fit** (how strongly the adjacency rules pull). Cascade, Wide and Village, and the Bright slider, were removed (they read as Terraced, Long bar and Compact, and as nothing); a project saved with them opens with the nearest kind. Seed, Min joint and piece scale are under More options. Every Generate picks a new seed unless one is kept.

**The plan** (`patterns.ts`). Pieces go on a lattice: 20 ft across the plan (the tile pitch) and **10 ft up**. Neighbours are level or 10 ft apart (a floor at 2 ft against a floor at 12 ft); pieces in one column are a whole tile apart (stacked); the entrance is the first cell, on the ground, and nothing is below it. Climbing is staggered: neighbouring columns take opposite heights, so each piece sits beside the floor of the piece next to it, never reached through its own roof. Every shape is built from those moves, and **Tall moves the plan itself**; at 0 every shape is flat. **Branching off** keeps the whole building the shape; **on**, the last third grows out of the core as wings.

The tall shapes are built round **a spiral climb**: every piece is one half-tile (10 ft) above the one before and winds round a hollow square, so the building rises with every block.
- **Tower**: the whole building is the spiral (2 x 2 pieces, 3 x 3 when Compact is low), as tall as it has pieces (16 pieces: 170 ft on a 40 x 40 ft footprint). Tall below 100 turns some pieces into rooms beside the climb; at 0 it is one level.
- **Compact**: a spiral up the outside of a small footprint with the rest of the pieces packed in beside it at the levels it reaches, so the mass is taller than it is wide.
- **Bridge**: a spiral up one tower, a level span across the top, a spiral down the other (Tall sets the height; the pieces left over lengthen the span).
- **Terraced**: a block a few rows wide and as long as the pieces need (about the square root of the piece count across), rising in plateaus of one to three columns; Tall sets how many levels (30 pieces: 40 ft at Tall 50, 70 ft at Tall 100). Each change of level costs one climbing tile, because a piece's way in comes from the piece beside it on its own level whenever there is one (so a wide terrace does not need a row of climbing tiles).
- **Courtyard**, **Long bar** and **Free-form** keep their own plans (a ring, a bar, a wandering mass).
- Searching also checks, as each piece is placed, that every empty cell that touches it can still be filled without a collision or a near-miss doorway; this is what makes a block of 30 pieces take about half a minute instead of never finishing.
Extra pieces (the rooms of a Compact mass, the wings, Free-form) are only put where some tile could actually meet the building there: the plan is tested with the look-ahead below as it grows.

**The search** (`generate.ts`). First a **look-ahead over the whole plan** (`planReach`): for every cell, which tiles can stand there and still be followed, cell by cell, to the end (a piece that must climb needs a neighbour that continues the climb; a cell that touches nothing else must have all its floors reached through the way in). It rules out in a moment what the search used to find out in thousands of steps, and it tells at once when a plan cannot be built from these tiles. Then, for each cell, it takes the tiles (in all 8 orientations) that can stand beside the tile next to it with a walkable crossing between floors, ordered by joint quality, the Program and Varied, with a little chance so that runs differ, and backs up when a choice leaves something that cannot be put right. Long plans are searched in stages of eight cells; the last four of each stage are searched again with the next. What it can stand beside is worked out once per pair and remembered (`pairs.ts`; a cheap exact test turns most pairs away before the joint is calculated). A look-ahead rejects a piece as soon as no remaining cell could supply a floor it needs.

**What every result obeys** (`auditBuilding` reads it back from the finished layout):
1. **Floors meet floors**: every joint on the way in is a walkable crossing between floors at the same height (within the step), scoring above Min joint (where openings and voids have to match).
2. **No dead-end stair**: a tile whose main floor climbs 6 ft or more has a neighbour meeting the top of the climb.
3. **No stranded floor**: every floor plate (30 ft² or more) of every tile can be walked to from the entrance, the upper floors that belong to a neighbour included. Smaller door-level pockets are not floor plates.
4. One way in on the ground; the Program's counts, limits, site and "never".

**What it can and cannot make.** Only tiles that lift circulation from the ground to the upper level inside themselves (in the current set G5, L1 and L5; L4 and G1 part of the way) let a building climb: a tile's upper floor that no stair of its own reaches is an island that a neighbour must supply, and a neighbour at a higher level is reached only through a tile that climbs. So towers and terraces are chains of climbing tiles with others on their upper floors, and the panel says what it came out as against what was asked (levels, height, footprint, stacked, different tiles, climbing tiles). A Tower is made of the climbing tiles over and over, which is why **Varied** cannot give a tower of sixteen different tiles: when the copies the variety setting allows would stop the climb, the same tile is used more often (never more than the Program's *Max copies*, which is off by default) before the plan is made lower. When a plan is proven impossible with the tiles at hand, the next one is a little less tall (the result says so); the rules about floors are never eased. A bank of other tiles changes what is possible; the first run in a session is the slowest, and it is quick afterwards.

**Grow more** adds pieces beside everything placed (locked pieces stay); **Regenerate marked** removes the branch past each joint marked bad and grows it back. Both use the same search and rules. Suggest next, Fill the gap and Auto replace (Help) use the one-piece candidate machinery in `candidates.ts`.

## Checks
`npm run check:arrange` (scripts/check-arrange.ts) runs on the 15 fixtures with no browser: shapes and seeds give one connected, walkable, overlap-free arrangement; never rules are obeyed; turning and mirroring a group are exact; the orphan check agrees with the real check and both repairs work; the composite holds exactly the pieces' cells and reads; smoothing and its approvals; suggestions and replacements are valid; the interlock runs; the pair matrix; non-cubic tiles; print labels. `npm run check:generate` (scripts/check-generate.ts) is the generator's own test: every shape builds the pieces asked for, Tall and Varied change the building, branching works, runs differ, Grow more keeps what is there, the counts are obeyed, Stop changes nothing, and no result has a stranded floor, a dead-end stair, a collision or an unreachable piece. `npm run check:interlock` is the test of shaped, interlocking tiles (below); `npm run check:tiles` assembles the tile set every way. Run them with `npm run lint`, `npx tsc --noEmit` and `npm run check:parity`.

## Interlocking, non-cubic tiles
Tiles may have L-shaped, stepped, notched or other containers. Arrange reads WHAT A TILE OCCUPIES, cell by cell, and never its bounding box alone.

**The three kinds of cell** (`lib/arrange/occupancy.ts`): OUT (outside the container, `mask.u8` = 0: nothing of the tile's, another tile may fill it), SOLID (inside, `void.u8` = 0: foam, floor plates, support branches) and VOID (inside, `void.u8` = 1: a carved room, passage, shaft, doorway). The mask, not `void`, decides what is inside.

**Collisions** (`lib/arrange/collision.ts`). The box is only the quick test. Where two boxes overlap, a cell claimed by both is: OUT of either piece (fine: this is how shaped tiles nest), SOLID on SOLID (always rejected, plates and branches included), SOLID inside the other's VOID (rejected: a neighbour may never consume a room or block a passage a tile was carved with) or VOID on VOID (rejected: rooms stay as the tiles define them). `CollisionPolicy` has the two switches (`consume`, `sharedVoid`). The warning says which: "152 ft3 of material collides", "132 ft3 of a carved space is filled".

**Contacts and joints** (`geometry.ts`, `joints.ts`). A contact is a patch of cells of one piece face to face with cells of the other, found from the container surface, on ANY surface (a notch wall, a step), not only the six box faces. A pair may meet in several patches; they are ONE joint with several patches. The four parts (void, floors, foam, circulation) are counted over interface cells only. A joint carries three separate things (`Joint.connect`): **contact** (the pieces touch), **void** (open space continues across it) and **walkable** (a person can walk it), with a reason in words when the second holds and the third does not.

**Walkable** (`walk.ts`, `lib/walking.ts`). One walking model for Arrange, the Analysis routes and the usable-space check: a standing place is a carved cell over material with 6.5 ft of headroom and a 2.5 ft clear width (a disc), a route across a joint needs a standing place on each side no more than ONE step (0.5 ft) apart, open space at the higher floor across the joint and clearance round both in the REAL world, and floors on each side that belong to the tile's own circulation (a zone below 12 ft² is a pocket, not a space). **A jump is never a route**: a bigger change of height is crossed only by real geometry (a stair or ramp that is part of a tile's floor); a vertical void (a shaft, any joint across a horizontal plane) is a view, never a route. Defaults are editable (Analysis → Settings → Walking rules; saved with the project) and every cache is keyed with `walkKey()`. `layout.reach` says how much of each piece's floor can be reached from the entrance.

**Connectors.** Two floors that are close but further apart than a step, across a clear doorway, are reported as **"needs a connector"** (a stair or ramp that does not exist yet): not walkable until one is built, listed as a warning with the rise it needs. **Build connectors automatically** (Program, default off) or the **Add a connector** button at one joint lays a wedge of foam in the lower piece's room (a ramp, or a stair), accepted only when the program's own walking rules agree (`lib/arrange/connectors.ts`). Only the intent is saved (`ArrangementDoc.connectors`); the geometry is worked out again from the pieces whenever they change. Not built: connectors across a horizontal joint, or between pieces that do not touch.

**Verified without a browser**: `npm run check:interlock` (fixtures in `scripts/fixtures/shaped.ts`, built like engine output and read by the app's own analysis): the L + cube nest, a stepped section with meeting floors, solid and carved-space collisions, floors 0.5 ft apart (walkable) and further (not), a low doorway, a shaft, a doorway into a closet, every orientation, mirror and shift, composite → tile → placed again, the Rhino mass mask and smoothing, suggest next, the snapper, the pair matrix, and the generator on shaped banks. The engine itself: `engine/headless/tests/t_shaped_tile.py` exports a real L-shaped tile that the app reads.

**Limits**: no tilt; scale is supported but off by default; a tile must be on the 0.5 ft grid and not a hex prism; vertical routes between tiles exist only inside a tile (a stair or ramp that is part of one tile's floor); tiny corner touches (under 1 ft²) are not joints; shared void is a policy switch, off. The generator builds on the lattice of the bank's most common cube size and leaves out tiles of another size; the one-piece helpers (suggest, fill the gap, auto replace) work with any shaped tile.

## Pictures, films, boards
- **Drone tour** (`lib/arrange/drone.ts`): parked outside facing away from the building, it swings round to the entrance, flies through the voids on a path that keeps 0.8 to 2.5 ft from any foam, visits every space, rounds corners, looks ahead and into the deepest open direction, tilts up in tall voids, follows climbs, slows for turns, leaves through the nearest opening and keeps flying straight out until the frame is empty. Out-and-back spurs are cut from the path. The Export window offers a **Full** tour or a short **Highlights** tour, and **Edit route** (reorder, leave out or add spaces). `check:arrange` fails a tour that spends a quarter of its time outside after entering.
- **Pictures**: the whole building from outside, close-ups looking through an opening into the space behind it, and inside views from places where the view takes in the most different blocks (`findViews` in `lib/arrange/views.ts`); one size, optional clear background. **Turntable**: the finished model turning once round from the current view.
- **Plans and sections on one board** (`lib/arrange/drawingBoard.ts`): title, key numbers, legend, a scale bar, every plan and section at one scale; or separate PNGs / SVGs. The board and the report take width and height independently (or the height follows the content). Text on every canvas export fits its box (`lib/textBlock.ts`). Colours (`lib/boardPalette.ts`) are saved with the project.
- **Mesh quality**: the live viewports show the engine's own meshes; pictures, films, turntables, board exports, OBJ and STL swap in smooth meshes rebuilt from each tile's voxels (`lib/fineGeometry.ts`, `lib/exporters/printMesh.ts`: cubic upsampling, surface nets, light smoothing; box faces stay flat), High by default, cached per tile (at most 32).

## What is not done
- The Level control is a row of chips that cut the model 4 ft above a floor; the model is cut but not capped.
- The mass sent back to Rhino turns floor plates into ordinary foam (the engine's plate objects are not rebuilt).
- Very large arrangements (over 6 M cells) wait for "Analyse in full".
- Not built: a gallery that ranks several arrangements (one is worked on at a time; saved arrangements and Compare cover it), a mesh cap on level cuts.
- `lib/exporters/csgFuse.ts` and `lib/sections/volumeField.ts` are untouched.
- Not run in a real browser: GIF export, the Boards tab round trip.
