# The Arrange tab (built 2026-10-05)

One building, composed from the tiles, judged as architecture, kept, edited freely, shown and exported. This replaces the earlier plan documents; where the build differs from the plan it says so at the end.

## The rules that shape everything
- **One connected, walkable building.** Every piece must touch the main set (the biggest group of touching pieces) and be reachable on foot from the entrance (a joint with an opening of 4 ft² or more meeting an opening). The generator cannot make an island; manual edits that would cut pieces off open a dialog (remove them too / re-attach them / cancel); Add as tile and every export are blocked while anything is detached or overlapping.
- **Free placement, smart snapping.** Positions are multiples of 0.5 ft (the voxel size), not locked to a 20 ft grid. Near another piece a moved piece snaps flush, then opening to opening, then floor to floor (floor 1 against floor 2 is fine), else the half-foot. **Lattice mode** locks to 10 ft.
- **Any box size.** A tile is a box of whole cells (`tileFt`, any width/depth/height; 20 x 40 x 20 or 20 x 20 x 40 work, tested in `npm run check:arrange`). Tiles from the engine should keep to multiples of 10 ft with 10 ft floor-to-floor so floors line up; Arrange accepts anything. Tiles at another cell size and the Builder's hex prisms cannot be placed (greyed out in the Bank).
- **Orientation**: mirror in X, then quarter turns about the vertical axis (8 orientations), plus optional uniform scale behind the Advanced switch. No tilt or wall mode.

## Checks
`npm run check:arrange` (scripts/check-arrange.ts) runs on the 15 fixtures with no browser: every shape and seed gives one connected, walkable, overlap-free arrangement; never rules are obeyed; turning and mirroring a group are exact; the orphan check agrees with the real check and both repairs work; the composite holds exactly the pieces' cells and reads; smoothing and its approvals; suggestions and replacements are valid; the interlock runs; the pair matrix; non-cubic tiles. `npm run check:interlock` (scripts/check-interlock.ts) is the test of shaped, interlocking tiles (Round 5 below). Run them with `npm run lint`, `npx tsc --noEmit` and `npm run check:parity`.

## How it differs from the plan, and what is not done
- The Level control is a row of chips (All, L1, L2 ...) that cut the model 4 ft above that floor; the model is cut but not capped.
- The mass sent back to Rhino turns floor plates into ordinary foam (the engine's plate objects are not rebuilt); its flat tops are kept in the voxels.
- The generator runs on the page (about 0.3 s per try, four tries); the whole-building reading runs in a worker. Very large arrangements (over 6 M cells) wait for "Analyse in full".
- Tests of the generator's shape settings check validity, not taste: the shapes are biases, not templates, and a bank with few openings cannot pack tightly.
- The legacy `lib/exporters/csgFuse.ts` and `lib/sections/volumeField.ts` are untouched.
- Not built: a gallery that ranks several arrangements (one is worked on at a time; saved arrangements and compare cover it), a mesh cap on level cuts, per-tab shortcut sets beyond the lists above.
- The drone tour is verified by `npm run check:arrange` (every shape: no sample of the flight inside foam, all pieces visited, 2.5 ft clearance), and played in the live viewport; the recorded MP4 itself was not watched frame by frame.
- Verified in a real browser (Chrome pane): generation, drag, box select, nudge, group turn and the orphan dialog, undo, add, add as tile (it shows in the Viewer), compare, level cut, measure, find views, walk-through MP4, turntable MP4, STL, OBJ, drawings, report, Rhino zip (also ingested by the engine headless), saved list, key bar. Not run in a real browser: GIF export, the Boards tab round trip.

## Pictures, films, boards (round 3)
- **Seed**: every Generate picks a new random seed (and turns the building a new way) unless a seed is kept (More options: typing a seed keeps it).
- **The best pictures** (zip) have three kinds with their own counts, one size, and a clear-background switch: the whole building from outside, close-ups at the openings on the outside (`exposed` patches), and inside views from places where the view takes in the most different blocks facing each other (rays through the voxels, scored by distinct pieces seen, depth and sky; `findViews` in `lib/arrange/views.ts`). Inside views use the tour look (void hidden, solid foam, a light at the camera).
- **Turntable** (the old "build-up", whose one-by-one placing was dropped): the finished model turning once round from the current view.
- **Text on every canvas export fits its box** (`lib/textBlock.ts`: wrap at words, break a long word, shrink to a minimum, ellipsis last; never squeezed). The report grows in height to show every space and every warning (no "+ N more"); the Analysis table and diagram images and the drawing captions fit the same way.
- **Plans and sections on one board** (`lib/arrange/drawingBoard.ts`): title, key numbers, legend, a scale bar, every plan and section at one scale with its caption; or separate PNGs / SVGs.
- **Colours** (`lib/boardPalette.ts`, `components/shared/PaletteEditor.tsx`): background, text, title, headings, sections, plates, branches, floor tint; saved with the project; used by the report, the board, the separate PNGs, the Viewer drawing export and (with "My colours") the Analysis images.

## Mesh quality in exports
The live viewports show the engine's own meshes (a 40-cell field, light and fast). Pictures, films (drone and turntable), the Viewer and Builder PNG and turntable, the Boards PNG and animation (150 dpi and up), the OBJ files and the STL files swap in smooth meshes rebuilt from each tile's voxels (`lib/fineGeometry.ts`, `lib/exporters/printMesh.ts`: cubic upsampling, surface nets, light smoothing; the box faces stay flat). Each export window has a Mesh quality choice (High by default, Maximum, Standard, or the original engine mesh). The meshes are cached per tile and at most 32 are kept, so repeated exports cannot pile up memory. In the smooth-model view of an arrangement the combined model keeps its own (voxel-grid) meshing.

## Round 4: films, pictures, board sizes
- **Drone tour**: parked outside facing away from the building (empty black), it swings round to the entrance, flies the tour, leaves through the nearest opening and keeps flying straight out until the frame is empty. No fade filter. `plan.pose(t, seconds)` takes the film length (it sets how long the opening turn lasts). **Edit route** (Export dialog): the automatic tour is planned first, then the spaces can be reordered, left out or added (`planDrone({ only })`); "Back to automatic" resets it.
- **Pictures**: close-ups look through an opening in the outside wall into the space behind it (scored on the depth and the blocks seen through the opening); inside views are scored on depth layers, blocks in view and far light through openings, from places along the flight that are inside a space.
- **Board sizes**: the plans-and-sections board and the report take width and height independently (or the height follows the content). The board picks the number of columns and one common scale that fill the shape (`drawingBoard.ts`); the report lays itself out at the page width whose natural shape matches (`report.ts`). The Boards tab already had independent width and height.

## Round 5: interlocking, non-cubic tiles
Tiles may have L-shaped, stepped, notched or other containers. Arrange now reads WHAT A TILE OCCUPIES, cell by cell, and never its bounding box alone.

**The three kinds of cell** (`lib/arrange/occupancy.ts`): OUT (outside the container, `mask.u8` = 0: nothing of the tile's, another tile may fill it), SOLID (inside, `void.u8` = 0: foam, floor plates, support branches) and VOID (inside, `void.u8` = 1: a carved room, passage, shaft, doorway). The mask, not `void`, decides what is inside: an exporter that leaves `void = 1` outside the container changes nothing (tested).

**Collisions** (`lib/arrange/collision.ts`). The box is only the quick test. Where two boxes overlap, a cell claimed by both is: OUT of either piece (fine: this is how shaped tiles nest), SOLID on SOLID (always rejected, plates and branches included), SOLID inside the other's VOID (rejected: a neighbour may never consume a room or block a passage a tile was carved with) or VOID on VOID (rejected: rooms stay as the tiles define them). The policy is deliberately conservative: the only way two pieces may share box space is through outside-container cells. `CollisionPolicy` has the two switches (`consume`, `sharedVoid`); the composite already unions such cells correctly (material wins over space) should a policy ever allow them. The warning says which: "152 ft3 of material collides", "132 ft3 of a carved space is filled". Valid nested pairs are listed as an info line ("nest").

**Contacts and joints** (`geometry.ts`, `joints.ts`). A contact is a patch of cells of one piece face to face with cells of the other, found from the container surface, on ANY surface (a notch wall, a step), not only the six box faces. A pair may meet in several patches; they are ONE joint with several patches (`Joint.patches`, each drawn where the pieces really touch). The four parts (void, floors, foam, circulation) are counted over interface cells only, so a big overlap of boxes or a patch that touches nothing useful earns nothing. A joint carries three separate things (`Joint.connect`): **contact** (the pieces touch), **void** (open space continues across it) and **walkable** (a person can walk it), with a reason in words when the second holds and the third does not.

**Walkable** (`walk.ts`, `occupancy.ts` `WALK`). A standing place is a carved cell over material with 6.5 ft of headroom and a 2.5 ft clear width (a disc, nothing solid in it). A route across a joint needs a standing place on each side within the level tolerance of each other (exact 0.5 ft, riser 1.5 ft, ramp up to the set rise), open space at the higher floor across the joint, clearance round both in the REAL world (a third piece beside the joint can take it away), and floors on each side that belong to the tile's own circulation: each tile's standing places form zones, a zone below 12 ft2 is a pocket, not a space. So an opening that merely lines up is not walkable; a doorway into a closet does not connect the tile; a vertical void (a shaft, any joint across a horizontal plane) is a view, never a route (a ramp or stair inside a tile joins its floors as one zone). These are proto-architectural tolerances, not code compliance. `layout.reach` says how much of each piece's floor can be reached from the entrance ("12 ft2 of 146 cannot be reached": another level with no stair); a piece whose main floor cannot be reached is unreachable.

**Automatic generation** (`generate.ts`). A piece is laid against an OPENING on the container surface of a piece already there (a box face, or the wall of a notch or step). The new piece's own opening (any orientation and mirror, any of nine lateral anchors, any floor level) is laid on the slot's plane, so its box may overlap the old one's. A candidate is kept only if no cells collide, the rules and limits hold, and a person could walk from a floor already reachable from the entrance into the new piece's main floor; the chosen piece is also checked against the arrangement as a whole (it may not take the clearance away from an older route). Scoring adds an "Interlocking" term (a slider, default 50): how much of the new piece's own surface lies against useful structure and openings, and how far it sits inside its neighbours' boxes; the overlap of boxes alone earns nothing. Search is bounded (700 tries per step, six seconds per run, caches for joints and floors); when the bank cannot satisfy the constraints the run says why ("66% met no walkable floor; 26% would collide with, or fill a room of, a piece already there"). Grow more, Regenerate marked, locked pieces, Suggest next, Fill the gap, Auto replace, the pair matrix, the snapper (it snaps to notch walls and offers the nesting position), duplication and the re-attach repair all use the same collision and walkable rules.

**Verified without a browser**: `npm run check:interlock` (fixtures in `scripts/fixtures/shaped.ts`, built like engine output and read by the app's own analysis): the L + cube nest (boxes overlap, solids do not, two patches, walkable, floors meet, composite holds exactly their cells, the notch's `void = 1` trap does not leak); a stepped section with meeting floors; a cube through the L's walls (solid collision, rejected); a block in a doorway (a carved room filled, rejected) and in the notch (valid, but it seals the doorway: touching, not walkable); floors 0.5 ft apart (walkable), 1.5 ft under a low lintel (not), 3 ft apart (not, unless ramps are allowed and the doorways are tall); a low doorway; a shaft between stacked rooms; a doorway into a closet; every orientation, mirror and shift of the nested pair; composite -> tile -> placed again, the Rhino mass mask and smoothing keep the container exactly; suggest next offers the notch, the snapper takes it, the pair matrix finds it; and the generator, with no position supplied, builds assemblies of 2, 4 and 8 pieces from a mixed gathering / office / lobby bank over five seeds each, valid every time, finding 27 nested fits in 15 runs. Pictures of every case are written to `$ARRANGE_OUT`. The engine itself: `engine/headless/tests/t_shaped_tile.py` exports a real L-shaped tile (`mask.u8`, `void.u8`, `plates.u8` on one grid, nothing in the notch) and the app reads that. Not run: the UI in a real browser (patches on the joint plates, the joint panel's three lines, the Interlocking slider) - the type check, lint and production build pass.

**Limits**: no tilt; scale is supported (nearest-neighbour on every voxel array, so masks stay exact) but off by default; a tile must be on the 0.5 ft grid (`cellFt` 0.5) and not a hex prism; vertical routes between tiles exist only inside a tile (a ramp or stair that is part of one tile's floor); tiny corner touches (under 1 ft2) are not joints; the generator never overlaps void with void (shared space is a policy switch, off).
