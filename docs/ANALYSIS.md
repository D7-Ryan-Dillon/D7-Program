# The Analysis tab, against the project's matrix

The authority is Assignment 1 Part 3's matrix (`lib/scoring/matrix.ts`, word for word): twelve descriptors, each with a qualitative criterion, a quantitative criterion and a precedent. Everything else says how the app measures or approximates it, and says so. The whole thing is automatic: loading a tile (or adding an arrangement as a tile) reads it against the matrix, suggests the criteria to carry forward, fills the comparison and the boards. Every control is an optional override.

## What each result carries
`lib/scoring/matrixEval.ts` `evaluateTile(tile, inputs)` returns, for each descriptor: the value with its unit and a one-line result; a **status** (measured, inferred, proxy, assumed, not assessable, not applicable); how it was measured (what was counted, the denominator, the thresholds); **what it cannot establish**; an automated reading (labelled generated, never a human judgment or a simulation); the assumptions it used; and evidence (rooms, levels, a route, regions) for the views and the diagrams. Missing information gives "not assessable" with the reason, never a zero or a pass; the rest of the workflow carries on. The app's older 0-100 blend is kept as a small "presence index", labelled as such; it is not the matrix measurement and is not used to choose criteria. Old scores are not relabelled: the twelfth descriptor is **Spatial density** (the quality the team once called compressed-then-released: "Perceived contrast between narrowest and widest moments", measured as the ratio of the narrowest to the widest passage width along the sequence). The earlier Spatial density formula (the void's cross-section *area* along the engine's main route) is a different quantity: it is kept as a labelled **legacy measurement** under the Spatial density card, and the current criterion is recomputed, never relabelled.

## How each descriptor is read (and what is only a proxy)
| Descriptor | Quantity | Status | Method in one line |
|---|---|---|---|
| Carved | how many carved spaces, how hollow the block is | measured | separate bodies of void of at least 20 ft³ (specks left out) and the chambers of the room analysis, whichever is more; the void's share of the container; the largest space's share; the share of the void's surface that is eroded foam is a supporting line |
| Stepped / Terraced | steps per 10 ft of height | measured | the most of: setbacks of the void's section, terraces of floors, **risers of the floor** (flat treads of at least 4 ft² joined by a step of 1 to 8 ft, read on the floor surface, walkable or not: a seat is a tread), floors that step up level by level, and floor plates standing at different heights |
| Porous | % of void wall area that is opening | measured | wall faces of the void with air beyond over openings plus solid wall (rough wall scaled to the smooth surface); bridges and cross-level void listed, not added |
| Continuous | % continuity of space | measured | the mean of the share of the void that is one connected body and the share of the walkable floor that is one surface; the room analysis (chambers, passages, loops) beside it; the older count of material changes along a route stays as a supporting line (it is not a seam count) |
| Retained / Resistant | floors' worth of retained plate, % of the footprint | measured / not assessable | the retained element is the recipe's floor plates (the ground slab, the plate that reaches the bottom of the tile, is the foundation and is reported apart); their plan area over the footprint, how much of the designed thickness survived, the recipe's resistance; volume and branches beside it |
| Threaded | walkable spaces above the ground floor | inferred | stretches of walkable floor on a level more than the ground tolerance above the lowest walkable floor, each a program instance of the tile's category (or, for an assembly, of the room it is in), public by the visible list; any room can be set by hand; the room analysis is the fallback where there is no walkable floor |
| Graduated | enclosure steps from the way in outward | proxy | enclosure class (open to the sky, partly open, enclosed) at standing places spread over the whole walkable floor, ordered by distance from the entries (2 ft bands, a lone band takes its neighbours' class); the changes of class are counted; private/public is not assigned |
| Non-hierarchical Circulation | independent routes | measured | the walkable floor cut into 5 ft stretches; independent routes (no stretch shared, by max flow) from all the ground-level entries to the far, middle and three-quarter parts of the floor, capped at the limit set; loops (independent circuits) and entries beside it |
| Force-driven | share of the dose in the largest source | measured / not assessable | the recipe's sources: the largest source's share of the dose, the effective number of sources (1 over the sum of squared shares), how many carry at least 5%; gravity, drain, modes and void per dose beside it; without a recipe nothing is inferred |
| Light-filled | skylight area / void floor below | proxy | roof opening (void with air straight above) over the floor of the rooms it opens into; unglazed in the model; a geometric indicator, not a simulation; an assembly recomputes it from the combined voxels, so a tile above removes it |
| Monumental | height-to-width ratio, void share of building volume | measured | tallest room's clear height over its narrower extent, with actual dimensions; volume over the container's own volume |
| Spatial density | narrowest ÷ widest passage width | measured / not assessable | clear width across the direction of travel, at standing height, along a floor-supported route, smoothed over 3 ft; constrictions and expansions located; does not assume more contrast is better |

A **floor-supported route** (`lib/scoring/voxelFacts.ts`, built on `lib/walking.ts`): standing cells (material under them, headroom, a clear disc round them) joined by steps no bigger than one riser, from the side of the container with the most ground-level opening to a destination (the middle of the largest other room, or the farthest point reached on foot). If there is none, the criteria that need one say so; no line through open air is substituted. A stair or ramp is a run of standing cells; **a jump is never a route** (see below).

**Non-cubic containers and assemblies**: everything is read from the cells of the container (`mask.u8`), so the notch of an L is open air, never foam or void, and never part of a denominator.

## The bars
Every descriptor card carries a **bar** (`components/analysis/MatrixBar.tsx`, drawn from `lib/scoring/bars.ts`): segments for the app's own scale words, filled to where the measurement falls (hatched and lighter when it is only a proxy or an assumption, dashed when it cannot be assessed), and a thin **fit** line for how close the measurement is to what the tile's typology prefers (the rule in Variants and Criteria). The **at-a-glance strip** at the top (`OverviewStrip`) shows all twelve bars at once; in Compare it puts the tiles side by side and rings the highest and lowest where two tiles are at least two bands apart. Click a row to jump to the card. The same bars are drawn on the Boards descriptor pages.

## One walking model (`lib/walking.ts`)
Arrange (joints, reachability, the generator), the Analysis routes (Graduated, Continuous, Non-hierarchical, Spatial density), the usable-space check and the assemblies all ask the same code what a person can stand on and where they can walk. A **standing place** is a void cell with material directly under it, headroom above it and a clear disc round it; a **step** joins two touching standing cells that differ by no more than one riser; a **zone** is standing cells joined by steps (smaller than the minimum space it is a pocket). Defaults: 6.5 ft headroom, 2.5 ft clear width, 0.5 ft riser, 12 ft² minimum space (proto-architectural tolerances, not code, accessibility or structural checks).
- **A floor jump is not a ramp.** No setting turns a height change bigger than a riser into a connection; the level-tolerance setting only widens what is *reported* as "needs a connector" (floors close in height but further apart than a step, across a clear doorway). A connector is never walkable and is never counted by Auto Generate. A real stair or ramp is a run of standing cells (slope up to about 0.5; steeper surfaces break the clear-disc rule), and only that joins floors.
- **The rules are editable** (Analysis → Settings: one editor, `components/shared/WalkRulesEditor.tsx`), saved with the project (`useWalkRules`), with their scope stated: they change every result that depends on them, and every cache is keyed with `walkKey()`, so changing one recomputes the lot.
- Visual paths (the drone route, the evidence lines) are drawn differently from **traversable routes** (the ones Arrange and Analysis count).

## Usable space (`lib/scoring/usable.ts`, `UsablePanel`)
Carved void against the void over floor that can be reached on foot; plate area against floor that is exposed and usable (and what is too narrow or too low, and what has no way to it); void pieces against floor zones joined to the way in; void links between levels against levels reached on foot. The thresholds are the shared walking rules' (above): the same as Arrange's, by construction. Problems are shown where they are and can be lit on the plans.

## The profile, the criteria and the comparison (`lib/scoring/profile.ts`, `lib/useEvaluation.ts`)
- The criteria carried forward are **suggested automatically** from the whole set (can it be assessed, how good is the evidence, does it tell the tiles apart, are the typologies about it, does it repeat another) and adopted at once. **No minimum number.** A tile's score is never used: criteria are not chosen because they make tiles look good.
- New tiles are read against the adopted profile immediately. If the tiles change what would be suggested, the change is **shown** (what to add, what to remove, why) and takes effect only when you press Adopt; every tile is then read the same way again.
- **Overrides** (all optional, each restorable on its own, all saved with the project): pin a criterion on or off, the reason text, an interpretation you wrote (per tile), any threshold or assumption, a room's class, the route a tile is read along, the tile picked for a typology and your reason, the typology's spatial intention.
- Variants of a typology (`lib/scoring/compareSet.ts`, `VariantsPanel`) are read with the same criteria and assumptions, and three things are kept apart because a bigger number is not better architecture: **strength** (how strongly a quality is present, with the scale word it falls on: descriptive, never "better"); **fit** (how close that value is to what *this typology* is about: a target range, "more up to a point", "less up to a point", or no preference at all where none can be defended); **usability** (floor reached on foot, void reached, floor zones and levels joined to the way in, always shown with any recommendation). The preference rules are **system assumptions** informed by the typology's name and category, labelled as such beside every comparison; each can be overridden per typology and restored. Nothing is stretched to the variants' own minimum and maximum, a missing result is never a zero (a criterion that cannot be assessed for one variant is not used for any, and is listed), and proxies and assumptions are supporting evidence that can add to a tradeoff but never decide. A pick is made only where decisive criteria the typology is about favour one variant meaningfully, with no usability disadvantage; otherwise the answer is a tie, a tradeoff written out, or "the evidence does not support a pick". The fifteen typologies are never ranked against each other.
- Older projects: pins and reasons you wrote are kept as overrides (including any note or pin made on the earlier Spatial density, which now sits on the twelfth descriptor); the old carried list is not converted (it was chosen from the older scores) and a fresh suggestion replaces it; a note says what happened, and the older Spatial density numbers stay a legacy measurement.

## Boards and exports
The Analysis tab, the Boards descriptor page, the Analysis sheets, the results table (CSV and image), the annotated diagrams and the arrangement report all read the same `evaluateTile` results (`lib/scoring/boardText.ts` writes the board text; `lib/scoring/exportResults.ts` the table and diagrams). **Analysis sheets** (Boards → Export): landscape pages on the board's background, 22 × 11 in by default (any size, any dpi), automatic: (1) typology, spatial intention and the matrix in its own words with what is carried and why; (2) measurements with units and status and the readings (yours marked ✎), several variants side by side; (3) evidence diagrams, the usable-space check, the suggested selection and its rationale. Each kind of content can be switched off.

## Tested without a browser
**The results table is editable** (Analysis → Results → *Edit the table image*; `resultsBoard` in `lib/scoring/exportResults.ts`, options saved with the project): which tiles and which descriptors; what each cell holds (a bar, the measured result, its status, the reading, how it was measured); tiles down the side or across the top; the size in inches and the resolution (22 × 11, 11 × 8.5 and TV presets); a title, tile names, category and type, grid lines, text size, a transparent background, PNG or JPEG; with a live preview. The CSV holds the same tiles and descriptors.

**One reading everywhere.** The overview bars, the cards and the Boards pages are all drawn from the same result (`lib/scoring/bars.ts` `barOf`): a descriptor that has a value has a bar, a word and a status, and one that has none says "not assessable" in all three, never in one place and with a score in another. "Not assessable" is for a tile that really cannot be read (no voids, no floor, no way in, no recipe for the force-driven reading). A descriptor the walking rules you set leave nothing to measure is read with the standard rules instead and marked as a proxy, so changing a setting never turns a whole tile into "not assessable" (`evaluateTile`).

`npm run check:analysis`: the twelve descriptors word for word; every ordinary tile reads with no manual input; unlabelled program, no plates, no recipe, no voxels and a sealed room are "not assessable" with a reason; every descriptor of the fifteen tiles is readable, with bar, value and status agreeing, under four sets of walking rules; the eight descriptors that used to read the same on every tile (Carved, Stepped, Continuous, Retained, Threaded, Graduated, Non-hierarchical, Force-driven) take different values across the fifteen first-set tiles (the stepped amphitheater reads as terraced, the cascaded plates as stepped with retained floors); a sealed room is still one connected space. 

## Limits
The Analysis tab's panels and the Boards sheet panel are checked by type, lint, build and the checks above, and the sheets were drawn and looked at in node; HANDOFF says which browser checks were run by hand. Seams, panels, glazing, program labels beyond the tile category, required sun-hours and pedestrian counts are not in the files, so Continuous, Carved and Light-filled are proxies and Force-driven reads only the recipe. Enclosure steps do not assign private and public. One entry and one destination are read per tile (the largest ground-level opening; override in Settings). The selection suggestion weighs only carried criteria that a typology's name makes relevant; it is a suggestion to look at.
